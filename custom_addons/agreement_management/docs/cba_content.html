ARTICLE 1 – PURPOSE AND APPLICATION

1.1 This Agreement establishes the principal terms and conditions of employment applicable to Indian Ratings and Petty Officers employed or engaged by the Company on vessels covered by this Agreement.

1.2 The Agreement shall form part of each applicable seafarer’s employment agreement and shall be read together with the individual Seafarer Employment Agreement (“SEA”), the applicable flag-state requirements, and any applicable collective or special agreement.

1.3 Where a mandatory requirement of the Maritime Labour Convention, 2006 (“MLC 2006”), applicable national law, flag-state legislation, or other mandatory international maritime instrument provides a higher entitlement or protection than this Agreement, the mandatory requirement shall prevail to the extent applicable.

1.4 Nothing in this Agreement shall restrict the lawful authority of the Master concerning the safe navigation, operation and management of the vessel.

1.5 The Company shall maintain copies of the applicable employment agreement and relevant collective agreements on board the vessel and shall make them reasonably accessible to seafarers.

ARTICLE 2 – ELIGIBILITY AND PRE-EMPLOYMENT REQUIREMENTS

2.1 The seafarer shall possess all certificates, qualifications, endorsements and documents required for the position for which the seafarer is engaged.

2.2 The Company may require a pre-employment medical examination by a suitably qualified medical practitioner in accordance with applicable maritime requirements.

2.3 The cost of a Company-required pre-employment medical examination shall be borne by the Company, unless otherwise permitted by applicable law.

2.4 The seafarer shall provide truthful information concerning qualifications, certificates and medical fitness.

2.5 Recruitment and placement shall be conducted in accordance with applicable MLC 2006 requirements and applicable Indian regulations.

2.6 No unlawful recruitment or placement fee shall be charged to the seafarer.

2.7 The seafarer shall be provided an opportunity to review the employment terms and seek appropriate advice before signing the SEA.

ARTICLE 3 – TERM OF EMPLOYMENT

3.1 The normal contractual period shall be {{contract_months}} months, commencing on the date of engagement recorded in the seafarer’s SEA.

3.2 The anticipated sign-off date shall be as recorded in the SEA, subject to voyage requirements, operational circumstances, repatriation arrangements and the terms of the SEA.

3.3 Any extension beyond the agreed contractual period shall be handled in accordance with the SEA and applicable law and, where required, with the seafarer’s agreement.

3.4 The Company shall make reasonable efforts to arrange relief and repatriation at the end of the agreed period of service.

ARTICLE 4 – PROBATIONARY SERVICE

4.1 Where permitted by applicable law and the SEA, a probationary period may apply to a seafarer during the first employment term.

4.2 The probationary period shall not exceed the period permitted by applicable law or the agreed SEA.

4.3 During probation, either party may terminate the employment in accordance with the agreed notice provisions, subject to accrued entitlements and mandatory legal protections.

ARTICLE 5 – DUTIES AND NON-SEAFARER WORK

5.1 The seafarer shall perform the duties appropriate to the rank and position for which the seafarer has been engaged.

5.2 The Company shall not require seafarers to perform work that is prohibited by applicable law or by a binding agreement applicable to the vessel.

5.3 Where seafarers are voluntarily required to perform additional work outside their normal duties, such work shall be appropriately compensated where required by this Agreement, the SEA or applicable law.

5.4 No seafarer shall be subjected to disciplinary action solely for exercising a lawful right concerning a legitimate industrial dispute, provided that the seafarer acts in accordance with applicable law.

ARTICLE 6 – HOURS OF WORK AND REST

6.1 The normal working week shall be 40 hours, normally comprising eight hours per day from Monday through Friday, subject to the operational requirements of the vessel and applicable maritime regulations.

6.2 The Company shall organize work so far as reasonably practicable to ensure compliance with applicable minimum hours-of-rest requirements.

6.3 Records of hours of work and/or rest shall be maintained in the manner required by applicable law and maritime regulations.

6.4 Emergency work necessary for the immediate safety of the vessel, persons on board, cargo, or other vessels or persons in distress may require departure from the normal work/rest schedule to the extent permitted by applicable law.

6.5 Following an emergency or other interruption of scheduled rest, appropriate compensatory rest shall be provided where required.

6.6 Drills and other mandatory activities shall, so far as reasonably practicable, be arranged to minimize disruption to statutory rest periods.

ARTICLE 7 – OVERTIME

7.1 Overtime shall be compensated in accordance with the applicable wage scale attached as Annexure A.

7.2 The agreed basic overtime rate shall be US$ {{ot_rate_hour}} per hour.

7.3 Any guaranteed overtime arrangement shall be specified in Annexure A.

7.4 Overtime shall be recorded accurately and verified in accordance with the vessel's procedures.

7.5 The seafarer shall be given reasonable access to the records concerning the overtime attributed to the seafarer.

7.6 Emergency work performed solely for the immediate safety of the vessel, persons or cargo shall be treated in accordance with applicable law and the applicable collective agreement.

7.7 Where overtime records are unavailable because of an established technical failure or other circumstances beyond the seafarer’s control, the Parties shall determine the payment due using the applicable contractual records, payroll records and other reliable evidence.

ARTICLE 8 – PUBLIC HOLIDAYS

8.1 The following days may be recognized as contractual holidays, subject to the applicable wage schedule:

  • Republic Day – 26 January
  • National Maritime Day – 5 April
  • Labour/May Day – 1 May
  • Independence Day – 15 August
  • Gandhi Jayanti – 2 October
  • Diwali – {{diwali_date}}
  • Christmas Day – 25 December
  • Other holidays specified in Annexure A.

8.2 Work performed on a contractual holiday shall be compensated in accordance with the applicable wage scale.

8.3 Nothing in this Article shall interfere with work necessary for the safety and operation of the vessel.

ARTICLE 9 – MANNING AND WATCHKEEPING

9.1 The vessel shall be manned in accordance with the applicable safe-manning requirements and relevant international and flag-state regulations.

9.2 Watchkeeping arrangements shall be determined by the Master having regard to safe operation, qualifications, fatigue prevention and applicable regulatory requirements.

9.3 Where practicable, watchkeeping shall be organized to provide an appropriate rotation consistent with the vessel's operational requirements.

ARTICLE 10 – WAGES

10.1 The seafarer shall receive wages in accordance with Annexure A – Wage Scale.

10.2 Wages shall be paid regularly and no later than the date required by applicable law or the SEA.

10.3 The wage components may include:

a. Basic Wage;
b. Guaranteed Overtime;
c. Fixed Overtime;
d. Leave Pay;
e. Compensation for contractual holidays;
f. Allowances;
g. Other contractual payments.

10.4 Lawful deductions may be made only where authorized by law, the SEA, or the seafarer in accordance with applicable requirements.

10.5 The Company shall provide the seafarer with a wage statement or equivalent record identifying the principal components of wages and deductions.

ARTICLE 11 – ALLOTMENTS

11.1 The seafarer shall have the right to arrange regular remittances/allotments to a nominated account in accordance with applicable law and Company procedures.

11.2 The normal allotment shall be up to {{allotment_percent}}% of basic wages, or such other amount as may lawfully be agreed.

11.3 The Company shall process authorized allotments within the required payment period.

11.4 The seafarer shall bear no unreasonable administrative charge for a mandatory allotment service.

ARTICLE 12 – LEAVE

12.1 On completion or termination of employment, the seafarer shall be entitled to accrued leave and other leave-related payments in accordance with applicable law, the SEA and this Agreement.

12.2 For contractual purposes, annual leave shall accrue at {{leave_days_month}} calendar days per month of service.

12.3 Any compensatory leave shall accrue at {{comp_leave_days_month}} days per completed month of service.

12.4 Accrued leave shall be paid or otherwise dealt with upon termination in accordance with applicable requirements.

ARTICLE 13 – FOOD AND ACCOMMODATION

13.1 The Company shall provide food and accommodation on board in accordance with applicable MLC 2006, flag-state and health and safety requirements.

13.2 Food supplied on board shall be of suitable quality, quantity and nutritional value.

13.3 Where accommodation or food is not provided in circumstances where the Company is responsible for providing it, an appropriate allowance or alternative arrangement shall be made in accordance with Annexure A.

ARTICLE 14 – MEDICAL CARE, ILLNESS AND INJURY

14.1 The Company shall provide medical care and assistance to seafarers in accordance with applicable maritime law, MLC 2006 and the SEA.

14.2 Where a seafarer becomes ill or injured during the period of employment, the Company shall arrange appropriate medical assessment and treatment as required.

14.3 Where a seafarer is medically unfit to continue service, repatriation shall be arranged where required.

14.4 Entitlement to wages, treatment expenses, compensation and other benefits shall be determined in accordance with the applicable law and contractual provisions.

14.5 Nothing in this Agreement shall reduce any mandatory statutory entitlement.

ARTICLE 15 – REPATRIATION

15.1 The Company shall arrange and bear responsibility for repatriation in circumstances where repatriation is required under applicable law or the SEA.

15.2 Repatriation may arise, subject to applicable requirements, upon:

a. expiry of the agreed period of service;
b. termination by the Company or seafarer in circumstances requiring repatriation;
c. illness or injury;
d. shipwreck or other circumstances making continued employment impossible;
e. other circumstances prescribed by applicable law.

15.3 The Company shall arrange reasonable travel, accommodation and related arrangements necessary to complete the repatriation.

ARTICLE 16 – LOSS OR DAMAGE TO PERSONAL EFFECTS

16.1 Where a seafarer's personal property is lost or damaged as a direct consequence of shipwreck, fire, flooding, collision or another covered maritime casualty, the Company shall provide compensation in accordance with applicable law and Annexure B.

16.2 Claims shall be supported by reasonable evidence where available.

16.3 The Company shall take reasonable steps to safeguard property left on board by an ill, injured or deceased seafarer and arrange its return to the seafarer or next of kin.

ARTICLE 17 – PIRACY, ARMED ROBBERY AND CAPTIVITY

17.1 If a seafarer is held captive or otherwise prevented from leaving the vessel as a result of piracy, armed robbery, hijacking or another unlawful act, the seafarer's contractual status and applicable entitlements shall continue for the period prescribed by applicable law and the applicable collective agreement.

17.2 The Company shall make reasonable efforts to protect the seafarer and provide appropriate assistance during and following such an incident.

17.3 Repatriation and post-release arrangements shall be handled in accordance with applicable law and contractual obligations.

ARTICLE 18 – DISCIPLINE

18.1 The seafarer shall comply with lawful orders, vessel procedures, safety requirements and applicable laws.

18.2 Disciplinary action shall be based on a fair investigation and shall comply with applicable law and the SEA.

18.3 Serious misconduct may include, where established after appropriate investigation:

a. theft or fraud;
b. violence or threats of violence;
c. deliberate serious damage to Company property;
d. serious breach of safety requirements;
e. possession or use of prohibited substances;
f. serious refusal to perform lawful duties;
g. other serious misconduct recognized by applicable law.

18.4 Nothing in this Article prevents the seafarer from exercising any lawful right of complaint or appeal.

ARTICLE 19 – TERMINATION OF EMPLOYMENT

19.1 Employment shall terminate upon:

a. expiry of the agreed contractual period;
b. lawful termination by either party;
c. medical circumstances requiring termination;
d. total loss of the vessel;
e. sale or permanent withdrawal of the vessel where applicable;
f. other circumstances specified in the SEA or applicable law.

19.2 Unless a shorter period is permitted or required by applicable law, either party may terminate employment by giving {{notice_period}} written notice.

19.3 Immediate termination may occur in cases of proven gross misconduct or other circumstances recognized by applicable law.

19.4 Upon termination, the Company shall settle all wages and accrued contractual entitlements within the applicable statutory period.

ARTICLE 20 – EQUAL TREATMENT AND HARASSMENT

20.1 The Company shall maintain a workplace free from unlawful discrimination and harassment.

20.2 No seafarer shall be unlawfully discriminated against on grounds including race, colour, sex, gender, religion, nationality, political opinion, social origin or other protected characteristic under applicable law.

20.3 Complaints of harassment or discrimination shall be handled through a confidential and fair procedure.

20.4 Retaliation against a seafarer for making a good-faith complaint shall not be permitted where prohibited by applicable law.

ARTICLE 21 – DRUGS AND ALCOHOL

21.1 The Company shall maintain a drug and alcohol policy consistent with applicable law, flag-state requirements and recognized maritime safety standards.

21.2 Testing shall be conducted in accordance with the Company's lawful policy and applicable requirements.

21.3 Disciplinary consequences shall be imposed only in accordance with the applicable policy, evidence and due process requirements.

ARTICLE 22 – SAFETY AND PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT

22.1 The Company shall provide appropriate safety equipment required for the seafarer's duties.

22.2 The seafarer shall use required protective equipment and comply with safety procedures.

22.3 The Company shall maintain appropriate procedures for reporting hazards, accidents, near misses and unsafe conditions.

22.4 No seafarer shall knowingly be required to perform work in circumstances that create an immediate and unlawful threat to life or health.

ARTICLE 23 – GRIEVANCE PROCEDURE

23.1 A seafarer who has a complaint concerning employment conditions should first raise the matter with the appropriate officer or Master.

23.2 If the matter is not resolved, it may be referred to the Company's designated shore-based representative.

23.3 Where the issue concerns interpretation or application of this Agreement, the Union may represent the seafarer.

23.4 Nothing in this procedure prevents a seafarer from exercising a statutory right to complain to a competent authority.

ARTICLE 24 – UNION RIGHTS

24.1 The Company recognizes the Union as the representative of employees covered by this Agreement to the extent recognized by applicable law and the applicable collective bargaining arrangements.

24.2 The Company shall not discriminate against a seafarer because of lawful union membership or participation.

24.3 Authorized Union representatives may communicate with covered seafarers in accordance with applicable vessel procedures and security requirements.

ARTICLE 25 – DISPUTE RESOLUTION

25.1 The Parties shall attempt in good faith to resolve disputes concerning interpretation or implementation of this Agreement through consultation.

25.2 Where the dispute cannot be resolved at vessel level, it shall be referred to designated representatives of the Company and Union.

25.3 If the dispute remains unresolved, the Parties may refer it to mediation, arbitration or another agreed dispute-resolution mechanism.

25.4 Nothing in this Article prevents either Party from exercising a mandatory statutory right or remedy.

ARTICLE 26 – GOVERNING LAW AND COMPLIANCE

26.1 This Agreement shall be interpreted consistently with:

a. the Maritime Labour Convention, 2006, as amended;
b. applicable flag-state legislation;
c. applicable Indian maritime and employment legislation;
d. applicable STCW requirements;
e. applicable international maritime conventions; and
f. the applicable SEA and special agreements.

26.2 Where two provisions conflict, the provision providing the legally applicable mandatory protection shall prevail.

ARTICLE 27 – TERM AND RENEWAL

27.1 This Agreement shall commence on {{effective_from}} and remain effective until {{effective_to}}, unless replaced, extended or terminated in accordance with its terms.

27.2 The Parties may commence negotiations for renewal or amendment before expiry.

27.3 Amendments shall be made in writing and signed by authorized representatives of the Parties.
